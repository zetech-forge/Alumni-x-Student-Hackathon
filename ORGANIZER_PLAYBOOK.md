# Organizer Playbook (Internal — not published to the site)

Draft planning content from the 30 Sept 2026 concept meeting, proposed for the committee to review, edit, and approve.

## Alumni roles — proposed structure

The meeting listed possible alumni roles (mentors, facilitators, judges, team coaches, industry connectors, career advisors, sponsors) without assigning them. Proposed split:

- **In-team coach** — the required alumni/finalist member of each team already plays this role; no separate recruitment needed.
- **Track judges** — at least 2 floating alumni judges per track (5 tracks x 2 = ~10), alongside each track's Technical Lead and one faculty/external judge, scoring against that track's rubric.
- **Mentors/facilitators** — floating alumni circulating during Day 1 Ideation and Day 2 consultations, not attached to one team.
- **Career advisors** — staff a short "Alumni Corner" during networking/closing on Day 2.
- **Industry connectors / sponsors** — alumni working at partner organizations (Ajira Kenya, Brighter Monday, Powerlearn Project, Arifa Lab, Totosci) invited specifically in that capacity.

**Recruitment target:** ~43 in-team alumni (150 participants / ~3.5 per team) plus ~10-15 floating alumni for judging/mentorship — roughly 55-60 alumni total.

## Judging logistics

With ~150 participants in teams of 3-4, expect 35-50 teams across five
tracks. At 5 minutes demo + 3 minutes Q&A per team, that's 5+ hours of
judging if run sequentially in one room -- plan **parallel judging**, split
by track, each with its own judging panel (track Technical Lead + floating
alumni judges + a faculty/external judge, per the Alumni Roles section
above). Reserve a separate room or clearly divided zone per track for Day 2
afternoon demos (see `schedule.md`).

## Alumni Problem Marketplace

Before the event, invite a subset of confirmed alumni (aim for 10-15) to
submit a short real-world problem from their industry -- financial
services, health, education, agriculture, logistics, software engineering,
cybersecurity, manufacturing, or public-sector services. Collect these via
[FORM LINK] by [DATE].

On Day 1 (9:45-10:30, see `schedule.md`), run an **Alumni Problem Lightning
Session**: each contributing alumnus gets 2 minutes to pitch their problem
live before team formation and challenge selection. Teams can draw on these
pitches when framing their SDG angle within their chosen track, alongside
the track's own pre-written challenges -- it doesn't replace the track
challenge banks, it adds real-world framing on top of them.

## IoT & Robotics: physical logistics checklist

Unlike the other four tracks, IoT & Robotics needs physical planning:
- Hardware kits: confirm what's provided vs. what teams bring, and
  inventory/sign-out process
- Power: extension cables, outlet count and placement in the breakout room
  used for this track
- Safety: soldering (ventilation, fire safety), batteries, motors and
  moving parts, wireless interference with other teams' devices
- Space: this track likely needs dedicated lab space rather than sharing
  the general auditorium floor with Software/Web3/ML/Cybersecurity
- Equipment responsibility: who's accountable for damaged or missing
  hardware, and the process for reporting it
