With ~150 participants in teams of 3-4, expect 35-50 teams. Demo slots run
in parallel across tracks, not as one sequential queue -- see
[Prizes](prizes.md) and each track's Rules page for how judging is split.

**Timing remains subject to organizer approval.** The draft 105-minute
finale window is fully consumed by 15 required seven-minute presentations
(105 minutes) **for presentations alone**. Team transitions, judge questions,
technical setup, demonstration delays and Finale deliberation require
additional time.

The organizing committee must approve **an expanded time allocation or a
different approved presentation arrangement** before the final timetable is
published. No replacement start or end times are assumed here. Once approved,
the timetable must account for Track-Level assessment, identification of the
top three teams per track, Finale presentations, score consolidation,
award deliberation, awards and closing, including any separate Q&A time.
The draft slots above do not establish that capacity. Track panels must verify
whether the one-hour parallel assessment window fits their actual team counts
and technical checks; pre-review submitted evidence and request a revised slot
if it does not. Do not silently shorten acceptance checks.

Stage 1 has **no slide-deck requirement, formal pitch or presentation points**.
The presentation requirements for
[From Prototype to Real-World Impact](real-world-impact.md) apply only to the
15 qualifying finalists in Stage 2: **seven minutes including any demonstration
and 8-10 slides including the title slide**. Follow the announced finalist
preparation and submission instructions; the code freeze is not a universal
deck deadline. Finalists carry their Track-Level average out of **100**
forward unchanged and add the Grand Finale score out of **30**. The combined
maximum is **130**, normalized as `(Combined Score / 130) * 100`.
