# From Prototype to Real-World Impact

This common mandatory challenge is assessed **only during Stage 2: Grand
Finale Pitch & Awards Judging**, not during Track-Level Judging. The top three
teams from each of five tracks qualify: **15 finalists**.

Finalists explain how their existing prototype could become a useful,
responsibly deployed solution. Distinguish demonstrated functionality from
future plans; do not claim a production deployment or real-world impact that
has not been verified. Web3 sandbox restrictions still apply.

## What finalists must address

1. **Real-world problem identification:** affected users, sector, context and
   evidence that the problem matters.
2. **Proposed solution and value proposition:** how the prototype helps and
   what differentiates it from existing approaches.
3. **Technical architecture and implementation:** components, data flows,
   technology choices and what actually works.
4. **Implementation feasibility and scalability:** resources, costs,
   deployment needs, adoption and realistic growth constraints.
5. **Challenges, risks and mitigation:** technical, operational, privacy,
   security and sustainability risks, with practical safeguards.
6. **SDGs and societal impact:** specific SDG targets, intended beneficiaries
   and potential adverse effects.
7. **Evaluation and success indicators:** measurable outcomes, baseline,
   evaluation method and what would demonstrate success.
8. **Future development and opportunities:** prioritized next steps,
   partnerships and what must be validated before real-world use.

## Agreed five-slide structure

The **recommended presentation duration is five minutes**. Prepare the deck
before the submission freeze; finalists use it in the Grand Finale.

| Slide | Title | Essential evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Problem Identification and Context | Users, sector, problem evidence and current alternatives |
| 2 | Proposed Solution and Demonstration | Value proposition and a concise working demonstration or backup recording |
| 3 | Technical Architecture and Technology Stack | Architecture, implementation status and justified technology choices |
| 4 | Real-World Implementation and Feasibility | Deployment, resources, scalability, risks, mitigation and future development |
| 5 | SDGs, Societal Impact and Expected Outcomes | Specific SDG targets, beneficiaries, measurable indicators and evaluation |

## Common assessment: 30 additional points

| Criterion | Maximum points |
|---|---:|
| Problem identification and sector relevance | 5 |
| Solution relevance and innovation | 5 |
| Technical architecture and technology stack | 5 |
| Implementation feasibility and scalability | 5 |
| SDG alignment and societal impact | 4 |
| Risk identification, safety and sustainability | 4 |
| Measurable outcomes and evaluation | 2 |
| **Total** | **30** |

Judges record awarded points and supporting evidence for each criterion.
Full points require specific, credible evidence covering the criterion;
partial points reflect incomplete or weakly supported evidence; absent or
unsupported claims receive zero. These points are **additional to**, not
deducted from, the confirmed 100-point technical score.

```text
Combined Score = Track-Level Technical Score + Mandatory Challenge Score
Maximum Combined Score = 100 + 30 = 130
Normalized Score = (Combined Score / 130) * 100
```

The Grand Finale determines **Overall Hackathon Winner**, **Most Innovative
Solution**, **Best Social Impact Solution**, and **Best Pitch & Presentation**.
There is no extra presentation category hidden within the 100-point technical
assessment or outside the 130-point combined maximum.

The organizing committee must confirm the finale running order, transition
and question time, tie-break procedure, and special-award selection procedure
before the judges' briefing. Do not invent additional points or unpublished
award formulas. See [General Rules](general-rules.md),
[Prizes](prizes.md) and the [draft Schedule](schedule.md).
