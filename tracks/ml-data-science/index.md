# Machine Learning & Data Science

Every Sustainable Development Goal runs on data: where a cyclone or drought will hit hardest, where malaria is still winning and which drugs are failing, how a field engineer fixes a machine without a stack of paper manuals. This track is about turning open data into systems that people can act on. Teams build one working prototype that combines **data skills, geospatial analysis, machine learning, software engineering and AI agents**, with a domain ingredient that grows with the challenge: climate science and disaster planning, epidemiology and uncertainty, or multimodal product knowledge and access control. The track has **three challenges**, so that every participant can take on a project that stretches them without being out of reach.

Participants should choose according to their current skills and the full [challenge requirements](challenges.md), not the difficulty label alone. Climate Sentinel expects basic Python and data-analysis knowledge, then combines weather analysis with its specified forecasting, mapping and advisory components. Malaria Atlas Lab needs stronger geospatial/statistical reasoning and model evaluation. Product Copilot expects confident application development and integration of speech, images, text retrieval and access permissions. Use the [curriculum](content.md) to identify preparation gaps; beginners do not need to study every advanced AI tool.

Python, pandas, notebooks, scikit-learn and geospatial libraries are useful for the analytical challenges; speech/OCR models, retrieval libraries and a simple app framework support Product Copilot. Equivalent suitable tools are allowed. Pretrained/local models can avoid paid AI services, but teams must confirm data, model and hardware access. No starter dataset bundle or runnable kit is currently published in this track directory; confirm any event pack with the Technical Lead.

Over two days, participants practice the full journey of a data project: finding and cleaning open data, joining it to a map or a knowledge base, building a model and testing it honestly, wrapping it in an AI agent that shows its work, and explaining the result to people who were not in the room. We pay particular attention to the habits that separate a good prototype from a lucky one: validating without leaking information, comparing with a simple baseline, reporting uncertainty, measuring who a model fails, being open about limits, and making work that someone else can reproduce. Every team ties its solution to at least one UN Sustainable Development Goal of its own choosing.

On Day 1 teams select **one challenge** and an SDG, load their data and get a working baseline. Day 2 is for completing the implementation, evaluating results and preparing technical evidence. Choose **Climate Sentinel** (Beginner), **Malaria Atlas Lab** (Intermediate) or **Product Copilot** (Advanced).

Every challenge is assessed out of **100 points**, with no difficulty multiplier. Approximately two to three judges may inspect functionality, code, data, evaluation outputs and documentation. Their independent scores are averaged to rank teams. Demonstrations and short technical questions verify the work; **no slides or formal pitch are required, and presentation quality earns no points**. The top three teams qualify for the Grand Finale. The highest-ranked team receives **Best AI, Data Science and Machine Learning Solution**.

The shared allocation is **30 technical methodology + 20 data quality/provenance + 15 evaluation + 15 responsible AI/safety + 10 reproducibility + 10 practical relevance/SDG alignment = 100 points**. Prepare runnable code/notebooks, required analytical or multimodal outputs, evaluation evidence and documented limitations. The existing challenge scopes and technical minimums are unchanged.
