# Machine Learning & Data Science

Every Sustainable Development Goal runs on data: where a cyclone or drought will hit hardest, where malaria is still winning and which drugs are failing, how a field engineer fixes a machine without a stack of paper manuals. This track is about turning open data into systems that people can act on. Teams build one working prototype that combines **data skills, geospatial analysis, machine learning, software engineering and AI agents**, with a domain ingredient that grows with the challenge: climate science and disaster planning, epidemiology and uncertainty, or multimodal product knowledge and access control. The track has **three challenges, one for each tier**, so that every participant can take on a project that stretches them without being out of reach.

The track is open to everyone, from people writing their first Python script to experienced engineers. Beginners should be comfortable with basic computer use and willing to learn. No programming experience or maths beyond school level is assumed, and the [course](course.md) brings a newcomer up to speed in about two weeks. Intermediate participants will get the most from some Python, experience with tables of data and maps, and a first model built in a class or tutorial. Advanced participants should be ready to combine speech, images, text retrieval, access control and evaluation in a single multimodal system.

Over two days, participants practice the full journey of a data project: finding and cleaning open data, joining it to a map or a knowledge base, building a model and testing it honestly, wrapping it in an AI agent that shows its work, and explaining the result to people who were not in the room. We pay particular attention to the habits that separate a good prototype from a lucky one: validating without leaking information, comparing with a simple baseline, reporting uncertainty, measuring who a model fails, being open about limits, and making work that someone else can reproduce. Every team ties its solution to at least one UN Sustainable Development Goal of its own choosing.

On Day 1 teams choose a challenge and an SDG, load their data and get a first working baseline. Day 2 is for building the rest, testing and pitch preparation, and it ends with live demonstrations and pitches. Teams are judged on technical rigor, relevance to the SDGs, responsible AI, reproducibility, creativity and their pitch. See [Track Rules & How to Compete](rules.md) for the format, allowed tools, submission requirements and judging criteria, [Content & Curriculum](content.md) for the pre-event learning plan, [Curated Resources](resources.md) for data and tools, and [Challenges](challenges.md) for the three problems: **Climate Sentinel** (Beginner), **Malaria Atlas Lab** (Intermediate) and **Product Copilot** (Advanced).
