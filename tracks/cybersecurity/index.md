# Cybersecurity

Build and verify defensive monitoring for a simulated organization's systems. Teams use Wazuh and supplied telemetry to detect suspicious behavior, explain the underlying weakness, apply appropriate controls and show that legitimate activity still works. Familiarity with Windows or Linux, basic networking and reading logs is useful; the [student guide](1_STUDENT_GUIDE.md) and [Wazuh manual](3_WAZUH_MANUAL.md) support setup and rule development.

This is a **task-based track**, not a Beginner/Intermediate/Advanced competition. Select **two or three of nine lab tasks**: five Windows and four Linux, using one platform or both. Preserve your rule/configuration files, alert exports, reproduction notes, benign-test results and prevention evidence. Security testing is allowed **only in the designated lab and on explicitly authorized targets**; university infrastructure, unrelated external systems and other teams' systems are out of scope.

Every team's selected task set is assessed once out of **100 technical points**. Approximately two to three judges may inspect your rules, configurations and evidence, ask technical questions and rerun a selected behavior with changed lab inputs. Their independent scores are averaged for ranking. **No slide deck or formal pitch is required, and presentation quality earns no points.** The top three teams qualify for the Grand Finale; the highest-ranked team receives **Best Cybersecurity Solution**.

Read [Challenges](challenges.md) for the task cards and [Track Rules & How to Compete](rules.md) for authorized scope, tools, submissions, the scoring rubric and tie-breakers.
