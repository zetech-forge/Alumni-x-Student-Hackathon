IoT & Robotics is about connecting physical devices to solve practical problems. Teams will combine sensors, microcontrollers, simple control systems, and clear user feedback to build a working prototype. The track is open to beginners as well as experienced makers; curiosity, teamwork, and a willingness to test and improve are more important than prior robotics experience.

Participants can practice reading sensor inputs, writing and debugging embedded code, controlling LEDs, buzzers, relays, and rover motors, documenting a system, and explaining design trade-offs. Arduino Uno R3 and ESP32 boards are available, but the physical component pool is limited. Teams may develop and demonstrate in Wokwi or another suitable simulator, use allocated hardware, or combine simulation and hardware. Simulation is a valid route to a complete submission.

Teams select one Beginner, Intermediate, or Advanced challenge and build a working prototype during the two-day event. The tiers range from a motion-based room activity indicator to an environmental alert monitor and a rover route challenge. See [Challenges](challenges.md) for the tasks and scoring, [Content & Curriculum](content.md) for preparation guidance, and [Curated Resources](resources.md) for learning links. The [Track Rules & How to Compete](rules.md) page explains submissions, safety, and judging.
